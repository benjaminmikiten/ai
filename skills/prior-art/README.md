# prior-art

`/skills:prior-art` as a plugin, or `/prior-art` with `setup.sh`.

When I've explored a problem long enough to know its frames and levers, I like to check whether I've just re-described something that already has a name. This skill does that backwards research.

It works in five steps:

1. **Distill** the idea into a structural brief: actors, mechanism, levers, tensions, boundaries. It writes the mechanism twice, once in your terms and once with every domain noun stripped out.
2. **Search by mechanism, not vocabulary.** Prior art rarely shares your words. An idea about sales comp plans is probably Goodhart's law; one about team boundaries may be Conway's law. The abstract mechanism finds the economists and biologists who named it first. It pulls candidates from at least three fields outside yours.
3. **Verify** every candidate by search, with a link. Inventing a plausible-sounding eponymous law is the main way this kind of task fails.
4. **Map the fit** element by element: same thing, special case, strong analogy, or neighbor. Every match has to say where it *breaks*.
5. **Extract leverage:** shared vocabulary, known failure modes, tools, and predictions to test your idea against.

The output ends with the **novel residue**: what's left of your idea once the prior art is accounted for. Like a patent examiner citing prior art, the useful part is often the claims it *doesn't* cover.

**Use it when:** you have a worked-out idea, framework, plan or strategy and want to know if it's new, or what to borrow.

**Setup:** none. Web search makes it much better.
